# Slate

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

React 19 and TypeScript, CodeMirror 6, IndexedDB, Canvas 2D, PDF.js, Mammoth, and a local visual math field. The shared offline application ships as Electron Windows/Linux, Capacitor Android, and an independent web build. Physical device verification remains open.

## Users

University students working daily with lecture notes, handwritten problem solving, linked knowledge, and course documents.

## Product Purpose

One calm local workspace for writing, studying, reading, and connecting ideas. No account or internet connection is required after installation.

## Operating Context

Desktop keyboard and mouse; Android touch and stylus; discrete notebook pages; Markdown remains the source of truth; imported PDF and DOCX originals are preserved.

## Capabilities and Constraints

Markdown live preview, source and reading; existing folder vaults; wiki links, backlinks, visual math/tables, universal optional line numbers, Vim and saved column layout; paged notebooks; pressure-aware ink, erasing, selection transforms, undo, pinch and two-finger pan; PDF annotation; DOCX reading; folders, tabs, local search, light and dark themes, backup and restore. Creation choices are Markdown and Notebook. Flexcil imports preserve originals and recover PDFs, with undecoded handwriting/audio clearly disclosed.
No AI, accounts, cloud sync, collaboration, recognition, OCR, social features or plugin marketplace.

## Brand Commitments

Original identity called Slate. Refined, calm, fast, restrained and consistent. Motion communicates state and respects reduced motion. No imitation of a competitor.

## Evidence on Hand

The user's specification is the product authority. The established Slate identity in DESIGN.md is binding. The root application is the sole maintained build; legacy prototypes are research only. Initial example notes are explicitly labeled editable examples; no performance benchmarks or physical-device claims are invented.

## Product Principles

1. Comfortable study workflows.
2. Local ownership and recoverability.
3. Reliable low-latency interaction.
4. Simple maintainable boundaries.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Keyboard access, semantic controls, visible focus, sufficient contrast, scalable text, reduced motion and generous touch targets.
